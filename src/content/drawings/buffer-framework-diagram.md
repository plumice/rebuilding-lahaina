The organizing concept of the recovery plan shown as a mauka-to-makai transect, with four buffer zones surrounding a protected urban core.

### The four buffers

1. **Coastal buffer** — Managed retreat, dune restoration, coastal park, and a pedestrian promenade replace the vulnerable shoreline edge.
2. **Peri-urban buffer** — An agricultural park and fire break between the town and the wildfire-prone hillside, combining food production with fire attenuation.
3. **Riparian corridor** — Stream and ditch restoration reconnects the water system from mountain to coast, providing flood attenuation and ecological habitat.
4. **Hinterland catchment** — Cloud forest reforestation and high-elevation storage capture rainfall before it reaches the town, feeding the water network by gravity.

### Three logics

Each buffer operates through **attenuation** (slowing environmental forces), **connection** (linking systems across scale), and **occupation** (programming spaces for daily community use alongside their protective function).
